    Section Section 2.4 Linear Equations

    Subsection Linear Equations

    Definition 71. Linear Equation.

    An equation that can be written as \(Ax + By = C\text{,}\) where \(A\text{,}\) \(B\text{,}\) \(C\) are real numbers and \(A\) and \(B\) are not both \(0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    In other words: Linear equations are equations of first degree. That is, their variables have no written exponents. We saw this in Section 1.1, but this time we have two variables. The graph of the solution set is a line, hence the name "linear".
    🔗
    There are two types of lines:
    🔗
    1. Vertical
      🔗
    2. Non-vertical lines
      🔗

    Subsection Vertical Lines

    Vertical lines are described by the equation \(x = a\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Slope-Intercept Form

    Every non-vertical line can be described with the equation \(y = mx + b\text{,}\) where \(m\) is the slope of the line and \((0, b)\) is the \(y\)-intercept.
    🔗
    The equation \(y = mx + b\) is called the slope-intercept form of a line.
    🔗
    Understanding Lines and Slopes Video
    🔗
    Definition 73. Slope of a Line.
    The slope of a line through \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) is:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{slope} = m = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The slope of a line describes its steepness and direction:
    🔗
    • A line with positive slope rises from left to right.
      🔗
    • A line with negative slope falls from left to right.
      🔗
    • A line with zero slope is horizontal.
      🔗
    • A vertical line has an undefined slope.
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Slope Examples

    Example 93.

    Example: Draw a line with each of the following slopes:
    🔗
    • \(m = 2\) (positive slope)
      🔗
    • \(m = -\frac{1}{2}\) (negative slope)
      🔗
    • \(m = 0\) (zero slope)
      🔗
    Solution.
    For \(m = 2\text{:}\) Start at the origin \((0,0)\text{.}\) Since the slope is \(2 = \frac{2}{1}\text{,}\) go up 2 units and right 1 unit to reach \((1,2)\text{.}\) Draw the line through these points.
    🔗
    For \(m = -\frac{1}{2}\text{:}\) Start at the origin \((0,0)\text{.}\) Since the slope is \(-\frac{1}{2}\text{,}\) go down 1 unit and right 2 units to reach \((2,-1)\text{.}\) Draw the line through these points.
    🔗
    For \(m = 0\text{:}\) Start at \((0,2)\text{.}\) A zero slope means the line is horizontal. Draw a horizontal line through \(y = 2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 94.

    Find the slope of a line passing through the given points.
    🔗
    1. \((4, -7)\) and \((2, 3)\)
    🔗
    2. \((3, -2)\) and the origin
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Let \((x_1, y_1) = (4, -7)\) and \((x_2, y_2) = (2, 3)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{3 - (-7)}{2 - 4} = \frac{10}{-2} = -5 \end{equation*}
    The slope is \(-5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    2. The origin is \((0, 0)\text{.}\) Let \((x_1, y_1) = (3, -2)\) and \((x_2, y_2) = (0, 0)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{0 - (-2)}{0 - 3} = \frac{2}{-3} = -\frac{2}{3} \end{equation*}
    The slope is \(-\frac{2}{3}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Writing Equations of Lines

    Find the slope and write the equation of the line Video
    🔗

    Example 95.

    Find the slope and write the equation of the line going through the given points.
    🔗
    1. \((4, 5)\) and \((-2, 5)\)
    🔗
    2. \((4, 5)\) and \((4, 8)\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Let \((x_1, y_1) = (4, 5)\) and \((x_2, y_2) = (-2, 5)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{5 - 5}{-2 - 4} = \frac{0}{-6} = 0 \end{equation*}
    The slope is \(0\) (horizontal line).
    🔗
    The equation is \(y = 5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    2. Let \((x_1, y_1) = (4, 5)\) and \((x_2, y_2) = (4, 8)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{8 - 5}{4 - 4} = \frac{3}{0} \end{equation*}
    The slope is undefined (vertical line).
    🔗
    The equation is \(x = 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    A line is uniquely determined by 2 points or 1 point and a slope. Writing the equation of a line in slope-intercept form can help you graph the equation.
    🔗

    Example 96.

    Example. Write the equation in slope-intercept form, then use the slope and \(y\)-intercept to graph the line.
    🔗
    \(2x + 3y = 6\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Solve for \(y\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 2x + 3y = 6 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 3y = -2x + 6 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} y = -\frac{2}{3}x + 2 \end{equation*}
    The slope is \(m = -\frac{2}{3}\) and the \(y\)-intercept is \((0, 2)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 2: Graph the line.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Finding the Equation from a Graph

    Example 97.

    Find the equation of the line shown below.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Identify two points on the line. From the graph, the line passes through \((-3, 1)\) and \((1, -1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 2: Find the slope using the two points.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{-1 - 1}{1 - (-3)} = \frac{-2}{4} = -\frac{1}{2} \end{equation*}
    Step 3: Use point-slope form with \((1, -1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} y - (-1) = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 1) \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} y + 1 = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{1}{2} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} y = -\frac{1}{2}x - \frac{1}{2} \end{equation*}
    The equation of the line is \(y = -\frac{1}{2}x - \frac{1}{2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Average Rate of Change

    Average Rate of Change Video
    🔗
    We have learned that the slope of a line can be interpreted as a rate of change.
    🔗
    For a nonlinear graph whose slope changes at each point, the average rate of change between any two points \((x_1, f(x_1))\) and \((x_2, f(x_2))\) is the slope of the line through the two points.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{slope} = m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{f(x_2) - f(x_1)}{x_2 - x_1} \end{equation*}

    Example 98.

    Find the average rate of change of \(f(x) = -x^2 + 2x + 6\) from \(x = -4\) to \(x = 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \(f(-4) = -18\) and \(f(1) = 7\)
    🔗
    This gives us the points \((-4, -18)\) and \((1, 7)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The average rate of change is the slope of the secant line through \((-4, -18)\) and \((1, 7)\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{7 - (-18)}{1 - (-4)} = \frac{25}{5} = 5 \end{equation*}
    The average rate of change is \(5\text{.}\)
    🔗
