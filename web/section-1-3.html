    Section Section 1.3 Complex Numbers

    Subsection Intro to Imaginary Numbers

    The imaginary unit is defined as:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} i = \sqrt{-1} \end{equation*}
    Therefore:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} i^2 = -1 \end{equation*}
    If \(b\) is a positive real number, then:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{-b} = i\sqrt{b} \end{equation*}
    Or sometimes it is written as:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{-b} = \sqrt{b}\, i \end{equation*}
    What were you taught? What do you see in the book? What’s the difference?
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Practice: Simplifying Square Roots of Negative Numbers

    Simplify each expression:
    🔗

    Example 13.

    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{-100} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\sqrt{-100} = i\sqrt{100} = 10i\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 14.

    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{-36} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\sqrt{-36} = i\sqrt{36} = 6i\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 15.

    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{-6} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\sqrt{-6} = i\sqrt{6}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 16.

    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{-20} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\sqrt{-20} = i\sqrt{20} = i \cdot 2\sqrt{5} = 2i\sqrt{5}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Practice: Multiplying Square Roots with Negative Radicands

    Simplify each expression:
    🔗

    Example 17.

    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{-16} \cdot \sqrt{-25} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\sqrt{-16} = 4i\) and \(\sqrt{-25} = 5i\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 4i \cdot 5i = 20i^2 = 20(-1) = -20 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 18.

    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{-40} \cdot \sqrt{-10} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\sqrt{-40} = i\sqrt{40}\) and \(\sqrt{-10} = i\sqrt{10}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} i\sqrt{40} \cdot i\sqrt{10} = i^2 \sqrt{400} = -1 \cdot 20 = -20 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Standard Complex Form

    Given real numbers \(a\) and \(b\text{,}\) a number written in the form:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} a + bi \end{equation*}
    is called a complex number written in standard complex form.
    🔗
    • \(a\) is the real part
      🔗
    • \(b\) is the imaginary part
      🔗
    Write the following in standard complex form:
    🔗

    Example 19.

    \begin{equation*} 3 + \sqrt{-16} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\sqrt{-16} = 4i\text{,}\) so the expression is \(3 + 4i\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 20.

    \begin{equation*} 5 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(5 = 5 + 0i\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 21.

    \begin{equation*} 7i \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(7i = 0 + 7i\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 22.

    \begin{equation*} \frac{6 + \sqrt{-8}}{2} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\sqrt{-8} = i\sqrt{8} = 2i\sqrt{2}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{6 + 2i\sqrt{2}}{2} = 3 + i\sqrt{2} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Powers of \(i\)

    Fill in the pattern:
    🔗
    \(i^0 =\) ________
    Solution.
    \(1\)
    🔗
    🔗
    \(i^1 =\) ________
    Solution.
    \(i\)
    🔗
    🔗
    \(i^2 =\) ________
    Solution.
    \(-1\)
    🔗
    🔗
    \(i^3 =\) ________
    Solution.
    \(-i\)
    🔗
    🔗
    \(i^4 =\) ________
    Solution.
    \(1\)
    🔗
    🔗
    \(i^5 =\) ________
    Solution.
    \(i\)
    🔗
    🔗
    \(i^6 =\) ________
    Solution.
    \(-1\)
    🔗
    🔗
    \(i^7 =\) ________
    Solution.
    \(-i\)
    🔗
    🔗
    What pattern do you see?
    🔗

    Example 23.

    What is \(i^{23}\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The pattern repeats every 4: \(i^4 = 1\text{,}\) \(i^8 = 1\text{,}\) etc.
    🔗
    \(23 \div 4 = 5\) remainder \(3\text{,}\) so \(i^{23} = i^3 = -i\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    There are many ways to do this. If you have a different method, go for it!
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Adding and Subtracting Complex Numbers

    To add or subtract complex numbers, add or subtract their real parts, and add or subtract their imaginary parts.
    🔗
    In the book, it shows:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} (a + bi) + (c + di) = (a + c) + (b + d)i \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} (a + bi) - (c + di) = (a - c) + (b - d)i \end{equation*}
    How does this compare to what you know about combining like terms of polynomials? How is it the same? How is it different?
    🔗
    Perform the operations:
    🔗

    Example 24.

    \begin{equation*} (8 + 3i) + (5 - 2i) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} (8 + 5) + (3 - 2)i = 13 + i \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 25.

    \begin{equation*} (8 + 3i) - (5 - 2i) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} (8 - 5) + (3 - (-2))i = 3 + 5i \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 26.

    \begin{equation*} (5 - 3i) + 7i \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} 5 + (-3 + 7)i = 5 + 4i \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 27.

    \begin{equation*} (-5 - 2i) - (5 - 2i) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} (-5 - 5) + (-2 - (-2))i = -10 + 0i = -10 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Multiplying Complex Numbers

    When we distribute a binomial times a binomial, we multiply everything in the first by everything in the second. We do the same thing with complex numbers.
    🔗

    Example 28.

    \begin{equation*} -4i(3 - 2i) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} -4i(3) + (-4i)(-2i) = -12i + 8i^2 = -12i + 8(-1) = -8 - 12i \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 29.

    \begin{equation*} (4 - 5i)(3 + 2i) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} 4(3) + 4(2i) - 5i(3) - 5i(2i) \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 12 + 8i - 15i - 10i^2 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 12 - 7i - 10(-1) = 12 - 7i + 10 = 22 - 7i \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 30.

    \begin{equation*} (6 - 7i)^2 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} (6 - 7i)(6 - 7i) \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 36 - 42i - 42i + 49i^2 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 36 - 84i + 49(-1) = 36 - 84i - 49 = -13 - 84i \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Multiplying Complex Conjugates

    Recall that conjugates can be written as \((a + b)\) and \((a - b)\) for some numbers \(a\) and \(b\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Recall the difference of squares formula:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} (a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2 \end{equation*}
    For complex numbers, the same pattern applies:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} (a + bi)(a - bi) = a^2 + b^2 \end{equation*}
    (Since \(i^2 = -1\text{,}\) the product of complex conjugates is always a real number.)
    🔗

    Example 31.

    \begin{equation*} (2 - 5i)(2 + 5i) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} 4 + 10i - 10i - 25i^2 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 4 - 25(-1) = 4 + 25 = 29 \end{equation*}
    Notice that the product of complex conjugates is always a real number.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 32.

    \begin{equation*} (4 - 3i)(4 + 3i) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} 16 + 12i - 12i - 9i^2 = 16 - 9(-1) = 16 + 9 = 25 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 33.

    \begin{equation*} 4i \cdot (-4i) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} -16i^2 = -16(-1) = 16 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Dividing Complex Numbers

    To divide, we need to create an equivalent fraction with a real denominator. From the conjugates section above, we saw that multiplying conjugates gives us a real result.
    🔗
    So, create an equivalent fraction by multiplying by 1, written as the conjugate of the denominator over the conjugate of the denominator.
    🔗

    Example 34.

    \begin{equation*} \frac{-2 + 7i}{2 + 6i} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{-2 + 7i}{2 + 6i} \cdot \frac{2 - 6i}{2 - 6i} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{(-2 + 7i)(2 - 6i)}{(2 + 6i)(2 - 6i)} \end{equation*}
    Multiply the numerator:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} (-2)(2) + (-2)(-6i) + (7i)(2) + (7i)(-6i) \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} -4 + 12i + 14i - 42i^2 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} -4 + 26i - 42(-1) = -4 + 26i + 42 = 38 + 26i \end{equation*}
    Multiply the denominator:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 2^2 + 6^2 = 4 + 36 = 40 \end{equation*}
    Therefore:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{38 + 26i}{40} = \frac{38}{40} + \frac{26}{40}i = \frac{19}{20} + \frac{13}{20}i \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 35.

    \begin{equation*} \frac{4 - 3i}{4i} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of \(4i\) (which is \(-4i\)):
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{4 - 3i}{4i} \cdot \frac{-4i}{-4i} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{(4 - 3i)(-4i)}{-16i^2} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{-16i + 12i^2}{16} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{-12 - 16i}{16} = -\frac{3}{4} - i \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 36.

    \begin{equation*} \frac{4 - 3i}{5 + 2i} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of \(5 + 2i\) (which is \(5 - 2i\)):
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{4 - 3i}{5 + 2i} \cdot \frac{5 - 2i}{5 - 2i} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{(4 - 3i)(5 - 2i)}{25 + 4} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{20 - 8i - 15i + 6i^2}{29} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{20 - 23i - 6}{29} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{14 - 23i}{29} = \frac{14}{29} - \frac{23}{29}i \end{equation*}
    🔗
