    Subsection Some Basic Things We Should Know About Applications

    Subsubsection Geometry

    • Area of a rectangle: Area = length × width
      🔗
    • Area of a Triangle: Area = \(\frac{1}{2}\) × base × height
      🔗
    • Perimeter of a geometric shape is the sum of the lengths of each side
      🔗
    • The sum of interior angles in a triangle is \(180^\circ\)
      🔗
    🔗

    Subsubsection Percentages

    • Percentages: Part = base × rate
      🔗
    • Base is always the original value
      🔗
    • Rate (percentage) is in decimal form
      🔗
    • Simple interest: Amount of Interest = principle × rate × time
      🔗
    🔗

    Subsubsection Physics

    • Distance = Rate × Time or Distance = Speed × Time
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Problem-Solving Strategy

    Problem-Solving Strategy 3.

    Follow these steps to solve application problems:
    🔗
    1. Read – Understand the problem. What are you solving for?
      🔗
    2. Define – Assign variables. Figure out how they relate.
      🔗
    3. Draw – If possible, draw a picture or graph to visualize.
      🔗
    4. Equation – Write an equation using the given information.
      🔗
    5. Solve – Solve the equation algebraically.
      🔗
    6. Check – Verify your answer works in the original problem.
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Triangle Angles

    Example 5.

    The sum of the measures of the angles in a triangle is \(180^\circ\text{.}\) What is the value of \(x\) in the given triangle?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Read – We need to find the value of \(x\) in the triangle.
    🔗
    Step 2: Define – The angles are \(40^\circ\text{,}\) \(2x^\circ\text{,}\) and \(90^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 3: Draw – Already given.
    🔗
    Step 4: Equation – The sum of the angles is \(180^\circ\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 40 + 2x + 90 = 180 \end{equation*}
    Step 5: Solve
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 130 + 2x = 180 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 2x = 50 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} x = 25 \end{equation*}
    Step 6: Check – \(40 + 2(25) + 90 = 40 + 50 + 90 = 180^\circ\) ✅
    🔗
    Therefore, the value of \(x\) is \(25\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection School Tickets

    Example 6.

    A total of 575 tickets were sold for the school play. They were either adult tickets or student tickets. There were 75 more student tickets sold than adult tickets. How many adult tickets were sold?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Read – We need to find the number of adult tickets sold.
    🔗
    Step 2: Define – Let \(a\) = adult tickets, \(s\) = student tickets.
    🔗
    Step 3: Draw – Not needed.
    🔗
    Step 4: Equation – \(s = a + 75\text{,}\) \(a + s = 575\)
    🔗
    Step 5: Solve
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} a + (a + 75) = 575 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 2a + 75 = 575 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 2a = 500 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} a = 250 \end{equation*}
    Step 6: Check – \(250 + (250 + 75) = 250 + 325 = 575\) ✅
    🔗
    Therefore, there were \(250\) adult tickets sold.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Farmer John

    Example 7.

    Farmer John has pigs and chickens. When he looks out into the barnyard he can count 12 animals and together those animals have 30 legs. How many pigs and how many chickens does he have? Solve without an equation.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Read – We need to find how many pigs and chickens.
    🔗
    Step 2: Define – Let \(p\) = pigs, \(c\) = chickens.
    🔗
    Step 3: Draw – Not needed.
    🔗
    Step 4: Equation – \(p + c = 12\text{,}\) \(4p + 2c = 30\)
    🔗
    Step 5: Solve
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 4p + 2(12 - p) = 30 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 4p + 24 - 2p = 30 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 2p = 6 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} p = 3 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} c = 9 \end{equation*}
    Step 6: Check – \(3 + 9 = 12\) ✅. \(3(4) + 9(2) = 12 + 18 = 30\) ✅
    🔗
    Farmer John has \(3\) pigs and \(9\) chickens.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Prepaid Phone Card

    Example 8.

    Lisa purchased a prepaid phone card for $15. Long distance calls cost 16 cents a minute using this card. Lisa used her card only once to make a long distance call. If the remaining credit on her card is $9.72, how many minutes did her call last?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Read – We need to find how many minutes Lisa’s call lasted.
    🔗
    Step 2: Define – Let \(m\) = minutes.
    🔗
    Step 3: Draw – Not needed.
    🔗
    Step 4: Equation – \(15 - 0.16m = 9.72\)
    🔗
    Step 5: Solve
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 15 - 0.16m = 9.72 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} -0.16m = -5.28 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} m = 33 \end{equation*}
    Step 6: Check – \(15 - 0.16(33) = 15 - 5.28 = 9.72\) ✅
    🔗
    Lisa’s call lasted \(33\) minutes.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Movie Theatre Tickets

    Example 9.

    At the movie theatre, child admission is $5.10 and adult admission is $9.50. On Saturday, 170 tickets were sold for a total sales of $1210.20. How many adult tickets were sold that day?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Read – We need to find the number of adult tickets sold.
    🔗
    Step 2: Define – Let \(a\) = adult tickets, \(c\) = child tickets.
    🔗
    Step 3: Draw – Not needed.
    🔗
    Step 4: Equation – \(a + c = 170\text{,}\) \(9.50a + 5.10c = 1210.20\)
    🔗
    Step 5: Solve
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 9.50a + 5.10(170 - a) = 1210.20 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 9.50a + 867 - 5.10a = 1210.20 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 4.40a = 343.20 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} a = 78 \end{equation*}
    Step 6: Check – \(78 + 92 = 170\) ✅. \(9.50(78) + 5.10(92) = 741 + 469.20 = 1210.20\) ✅
    🔗
    There were \(78\) adult tickets sold.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Time, Rate and Distance

    Example 10.

    Two passengers leave the airport at Kansas City, Missouri. One flies to Los Angeles, California in \(3\) hours and the other flies the opposite direction to New York City in \(2\) hours. With prevailing westerly winds, the speed of the plane to NYC is \(60\) mph faster than the plane to LA. If the total distance travelled by both planes is \(2464\) miles, what is the speed of each plane?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Read – We need to find the speed of each plane.
    🔗
    Step 2: Define – Let \(x\) = speed of plane to LA, \(x + 60\) = speed of plane to NYC.
    🔗
    Step 3: Draw – Not needed.
    🔗
    Step 4: Equation – Distance = Rate × Time. Total distance = \(2464\) miles.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 3x + 2(x + 60) = 2464 \end{equation*}
    Step 5: Solve
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 3x + 2x + 120 = 2464 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 5x + 120 = 2464 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 5x = 2344 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} x = 468.8 \end{equation*}
    Plane to LA: \(468.8\) mph
    🔗
    Plane to NYC: \(468.8 + 60 = 528.8\) mph
    🔗
    Step 6: Check – \(3(468.8) + 2(528.8) = 1406.4 + 1057.6 = 2464\) ✅
    🔗
    Therefore, the plane to LA travels at \(468.8\) mph, and the plane to NYC travels at \(528.8\) mph.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Percentage Applications

    Example 11.

    Tasha considers two sales jobs for two pharmaceutical companies. One pays a base salary of \(\$25,000\) with a \(16\%\) commission on sales. The other pays \(\$30,000\) with a \(15\%\) commission on sales.
    🔗
    a) Write equations that represent the salaries of the two offers.
    🔗
    b) For how much in sales will the two jobs result in the equal salaries?
    🔗
    c) If Tasha expects to sell \(\$700,000\text{,}\) which job would be better?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Read – We need to compare two job offers.
    🔗
    Step 2: Define – Let \(x\) = amount of sales.
    🔗
    Step 3: Draw – Not needed.
    🔗
    Step 4: Equation
    🔗
    a) Job 1: \(S_1 = 25000 + 0.16x\text{,}\) Job 2: \(S_2 = 30000 + 0.15x\)
    🔗
    b) \(S_1 = S_2\)
    🔗
    Step 5: Solve
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 25000 + 0.16x = 30000 + 0.15x \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 0.01x = 5000 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} x = 500,000 \end{equation*}
    c) For \(x = 700,000\text{:}\)
    🔗
    Job 1: \(25000 + 0.16(700000) = 137,000\)
    🔗
    Job 2: \(30000 + 0.15(700000) = 135,000\)
    🔗
    Step 6: Check – \(137,000 > 135,000\text{,}\) so Job 1 is better.
    🔗
    a) \(S_1 = 25000 + 0.16x\text{,}\) \(S_2 = 30000 + 0.15x\)
    🔗
    b) Equal at \(\$500,000\) in sales.
    🔗
    c) Job 1 is better for \(\$700,000\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Combined Work

    Example 12.

    Malcolm and Janay have been offered \(\$100\) if they can clean out a garage in under 2 hours. Malcolm can clean the garage in 3 hours and Janay can clean the garage in 5 hours. Working together, can they clean the garage in time to make \(\$100\text{?}\)
    🔗
    What are their speeds alone? What is their speed when they work together? How much will they get done in 2 hours? Will they earn the \(\$100\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Read – We need to determine if they can clean the garage in under 2 hours.
    🔗
    Step 2: Define – Malcolm’s rate = \(\frac{1}{3}\) garage/hour, Janay’s rate = \(\frac{1}{5}\) garage/hour.
    🔗
    Step 3: Draw – Not needed.
    🔗
    Step 4: Equation – Combined rate = \(\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{5}\)
    🔗
    Step 5: Solve
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{Combined rate} = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{5} = \frac{8}{15} \end{equation*}
    In 2 hours: \(2 \times \frac{8}{15} = \frac{16}{15} \approx 1.067\) garages.
    🔗
    Step 6: Check – \(\frac{16}{15} > 1\text{,}\) so they finish in under 2 hours.
    🔗
    Malcolm and Janay will earn the \(\$100\text{.}\)
