    Section Section 1.5 Problem Solving and Quadratic Applications

    Subsection Problem Solving

    The study of mathematics is not simply to earn enough credit in the course to receive a passing grade and then quickly forget the material after the final examination.
    🔗
    Instead, the purpose is to develop skills for problem solving and occasionally discover the means for independent thinking.
    🔗
    George Pólya (1887–1985) was a professor of mathematics at Stanford University. In his book, How to Solve It, he offers a method of attacking any problem that can be reasoned out. He proposed 4 steps in solving a problem. The following 6-step strategy is an expanded, more concrete version of Pólya’s framework.
    🔗
    Quadratic Application Problems Video
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Problem-Solving Strategy

    Problem-Solving Strategy 9.

    Follow these steps to solve application problems:
    🔗
    1. Read – Understand the problem. What are you solving for?
      🔗
    2. Define – Assign variables. Figure out how they relate.
      🔗
    3. Draw – If possible, draw a picture or graph to visualize.
      🔗
    4. Equation – Write an equation using the given information.
      🔗
    5. Solve – Solve the equation algebraically (using factoring, quadratic formula, etc.).
      🔗
    6. Check – Verify your answer works in the original problem.
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 51. Ball Height.

    A ball is thrown from an initial height of 7 feet with an initial upward velocity of 21 ft/s. The ball’s height \(h\) (in feet) after \(t\) seconds is given by the following.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} h = 7 + 21t - 16t^2 \end{equation*}
    Find all values of \(t\) for which the ball’s height is 13 feet. Round your answer(s) to the nearest hundredth.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Read – We are given the formula \(h = 7 + 21t - 16t^2\text{.}\) We need to find \(t\) when \(h = 13\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 2: Define – Let \(t\) be the time in seconds when the ball’s height is 13 feet.
    🔗
    Step 3: Draw – The graph above shows the projectile motion of the ball.
    🔗
    Step 4: Equation – Substitute \(h = 13\) into the formula:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 13 = 7 + 21t - 16t^2 \end{equation*}
    Subtract 13 from both sides:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 0 = -6 + 21t - 16t^2 \end{equation*}
    Rearrange in standard form:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 16t^2 - 21t + 6 = 0 \end{equation*}
    Step 5: Solve – Use the quadratic formula with \(a = 16\text{,}\) \(b = -21\text{,}\) \(c = 6\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} t = \frac{21 \pm \sqrt{(-21)^2 - 4(16)(6)}}{2(16)} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} t = \frac{21 \pm \sqrt{441 - 384}}{32} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} t = \frac{21 \pm \sqrt{57}}{32} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} t \approx \frac{21 \pm 7.55}{32} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} t \approx 0.42 \quad \text{or} \quad t \approx 0.89 \end{equation*}
    Step 6: Check – The ball reaches 13 feet at \(t \approx 0.42\) seconds on the way up, and again at \(t \approx 0.89\) seconds on the way down. Both answers are reasonable.
    🔗
    Therefore, the ball’s height is 13 feet at \(t \approx 0.42\) or \(t \approx 0.89\) seconds.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 52. Fish Tank Dimensions.

    Dmitri is having a fish tank constructed to fit in a niche in his wall. The width must be 12 inches, while the length is twice the height decreased by 8 inches. The total volume of the tank must be 6840 cubic inches. What are the dimensions of the tank?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Read – We need to find the dimensions of the fish tank.
    🔗
    Step 2: Define – Let \(h\) be the height of the tank.
    🔗
    • Width (\(w\)) = 12 inches
      🔗
    • Length (\(l\)) = \(2h - 8\) (twice the height decreased by 8)
      🔗
    • Volume (\(V\)) = 6840 cubic inches
      🔗
    Step 3: Draw – The diagram above shows the fish tank with labeled dimensions.
    🔗
    Step 4: Equation – Volume formula for a rectangular prism: \(V = l \times w \times h\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 6840 = (2h - 8) \times 12 \times h \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 6840 = 24h^2 - 96h \end{equation*}
    Rewrite as a quadratic equation:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 0 = 24h^2 - 96h - 6840 \end{equation*}
    Divide both sides by 24:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 0 = h^2 - 4h - 285 \end{equation*}
    Step 5: Solve – Factor:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 0 = (h - 19)(h + 15) \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} h = 19 \quad \text{or} \quad h = -15 \end{equation*}
    Since height cannot be negative, \(h = 19\) inches.
    🔗
    Now find the length:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} l = 2h - 8 = 2(19) - 8 = 38 - 8 = 30 \end{equation*}
    Step 6: Check – Check the volume: \(V = 30 \times 12 \times 19 = 6840\) cubic inches. ✅
    🔗
    Therefore, the dimensions of the tank are:
    🔗
    • Width: 12 inches
      🔗
    • Length: 30 inches
      🔗
    • Height: 19 inches
      🔗
