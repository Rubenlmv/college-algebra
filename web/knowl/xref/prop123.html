Proposition 144.

Let \(p\) be a prime and let all matrices be over \(\mathbb{F}_p\text{.}\) Suppose \(\bar{y}=e_{1p}\) and \(\bar{x}\) are such that
\begin{equation*} \bar{x}\bar{y}=0=\bar{y}\bar{x}. \end{equation*}
If there exist \(A, B \in M_p(\mathbb{F}_p)\) such that
\begin{equation*} [\bar{x}, B] + [A, \bar{y}] = I_p, \end{equation*}
with the conditions
\begin{equation*} B e_p = 0, \qquad \operatorname{Im}(x_c b_r)\subset \mathbb{F}_pe_1 \text{ (this is equivalent to } b_r=0 \text{ or } x_c\in\mathbb{F}_p e_1\text{)}. \end{equation*}
where
  • \(b_r\) is the last row of \(B\) (restricted to its first \(p-1\) entries),
    🔗
  • \(x_c\) is the last column of \(\bar{x}\) (restricted to its first \(p-1\) entries),
    🔗
  • \(x_m\) is the top-left \((p-1)\times(p-1)\) submatrix of \(\bar{x}\text{,}\)
    🔗
  • \(b_m\) is the top-left \((p-1)\times(p-1)\) submatrix of \(B\text{,}\)
    🔗
then \((x_m, b_m)\) is a Calogero-Moser pair.
🔗
Conversely, if \((x_m, b_m)\) is a Calogero-Moser pair such that
\begin{equation*} [x_m, b_m] = I_{p-1} + R \end{equation*}
with \(\operatorname{Im}(R) \subseteq \mathbb{F}_p e_1\) and \(x_m e_1 = 0\text{,}\) then for any \(b_r \in \mathbb{F}_p^{\,1\times(p-1)}\) and \(x_c \in \mathbb{F}_p^{(p-1)\times 1}\) satisfying
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{Im}(x_c b_r)\subset \mathbb{F}_pe_1, \end{equation*}
there exists \(A \in M_p(\mathbb{F}_p)\) such that
\begin{equation*} [\bar{x}, B] + [A, \bar{y}] = I_p. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Sketch of the proof:
🔗
Block notation. Write
\begin{equation*} \bar{x} = \begin{pmatrix} x_m & x_c \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} b_m & b \\ b_r & 0 \end{pmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Set
\begin{equation*} X_m = \begin{pmatrix} x_m & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B_m = \begin{pmatrix} b_m & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} X_c = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & x_c \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B_r = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ b_r & 0 \end{pmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Then we have the following commutators:
\begin{equation*} [B_r, X_m] = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ b_r x_m & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} [B_m, X_c] = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & b_m x_c \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad [B_r, X_c] = \begin{pmatrix} -x_c b_r & 0 \\ 0 & b_r x_c \end{pmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
In addition
\begin{equation*} [A,\bar{y}] = \begin{pmatrix} -a_{p1} & -a_{p2} & \cdots & a_{11}-a_{pp} \\ 0 & \cdots & \cdots & a_{21} \\ \vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & a_{p1} \end{pmatrix}, \end{equation*}
with
\begin{equation*} x_c = \begin{pmatrix} x_{1p} \\ x_{2p} \\ \vdots \\ x_{p-1,p} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad b_r = \begin{pmatrix} b_{p1} & \cdots & b_{p,p-1} \end{pmatrix}. \end{equation*}
Moreover,
\begin{equation*} x_c b_r = \bigl( x_{ip}\, b_{pj} \bigr)_{i,j=1}^{p-1}, \qquad b_r x_c = \sum_{i=1}^{p-1} b_{pi}\, x_{ip}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Now, since \(\bar{x} = X_m + X_c\) and \(B = B_m + B_r\text{,}\) we have
\begin{equation*} [\bar{x}, B] = [X_m + X_c, B_m + B_r] = [X_m, B_m] + [X_m, B_r] + [X_c, B_m] + [X_c, B_r]. \end{equation*}
🔗
Using the explicit block commutators, the equation
\begin{equation*} [\bar{x}, B] + [A, \bar{y}] = I_p \end{equation*}
is equivalent to
\begin{equation*} [X_m, B_m] = I_p - [A,\bar{y}] + \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ b_r x_m & 0 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 0 & b_m x_c \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} -x_c b_r & 0 \\ 0 & b_r x_c \end{pmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Taking the top-left \((p-1)\times(p-1)\) block gives
\begin{equation*} [x_m, b_m] = I_{p-1} + \begin{pmatrix} a_{p1} & a_{p2} & \cdots & a_{p,p-1} \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \end{pmatrix} - x_c b_r. \end{equation*}
🔗
This yields the Calogero-Moser condition for \((x_m,b_m)\text{,}\) precisely when \(\operatorname{Im}(x_c b_r)\subset \mathbb{F}_pe_1\text{.}\)
