    Section Section 2.1 Rectangular Coordinate System

    Subsection Rectangular Coordinate System

    Also called the Cartesian Coordinate System, named after René Descartes, who first proposed identifying points in a plane by using two perpendicular number lines, intersecting at \(0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Plotting Points

    Every point can be identified by an ordered pair, given as \((x, y)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Subsection Distance Formula

    Distance Formula Video
    🔗
    The distance formula between two points can be found using the Pythagorean Theorem.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2} \end{equation*}

    Example 76.

    Example. Find the distance between the points \((-3, -4)\) and \((3, 0)\text{.}\) Give the exact distance and an approximation to 2 decimal places.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Identify the coordinates.
    🔗
    Let \((x_1, y_1) = (-3, -4)\) and \((x_2, y_2) = (3, 0)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 2: Apply the distance formula.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} d = \sqrt{(3 - (-3))^2 + (0 - (-4))^2} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} d = \sqrt{6^2 + 4^2} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} d = \sqrt{36 + 16} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} d = \sqrt{52} = 2\sqrt{13} \end{equation*}
    Step 3: Approximate to 2 decimal places.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} d \approx 7.21 \end{equation*}
    Therefore, the exact distance is \(2\sqrt{13}\) and the approximate distance is \(7.21\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Midpoint Formula

    Midpoint Formula Video
    🔗
    The midpoint of the line segment with endpoints \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) is:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right) \end{equation*}

    Example 77.

    Example. Find the midpoint of the line segment with endpoints \((-1.5, -3)\) and \((4.5, 5)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Identify the coordinates.
    🔗
    Let \((x_1, y_1) = (-1.5, -3)\) and \((x_2, y_2) = (4.5, 5)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 2: Apply the midpoint formula.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \left(\frac{-1.5 + 4.5}{2}, \frac{-3 + 5}{2}\right) \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \left(\frac{3}{2}, \frac{2}{2}\right) \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} (1.5, 1) \end{equation*}
    Therefore, the midpoint is \((1.5, 1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Identifying x- and y- Intercepts

    x- and y- Intercepts Video
    🔗

    Problem-Solving Strategy 47. Finding x- and y-intercepts.

    1. To find the \(x\)-intercept(s), substitute \(y=0\) into the equation and solve for \(x\text{.}\)
      🔗
    2. To find the \(y\)-intercept(s), substitute \(x=0\) into the equation and solve for \(y\text{.}\)
      🔗
    🔗
    Any point on the \(x\)-axis has \(0\) as its \(y\)-coordinate: \((a, 0)\text{.}\) Any point on the \(y\)-axis has \(0\) as its \(x\)-coordinate: \((0, b)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Example 80.

    Estimate the \(x\)- and \(y\)-intercepts from the graph of \(y = x^2 - 4\) shown below.
    🔗
    Solution.
    From the graph, the parabola crosses the \(x\)-axis at \((-2,0)\) and \((2,0)\text{,}\) and the \(y\)-axis at \((0,-4)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 81.

    Find the \(x\)- and \(y\)-intercepts of the circle \((x + 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 16\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Find the y-intercepts: Set \(x = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} (0 + 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 16 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 9 + (y - 2)^2 = 16 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} (y - 2)^2 = 7 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} y = 2 \pm \sqrt{7} \end{equation*}
    y-intercepts: \((0, 2 + \sqrt{7})\) and \((0, 2 - \sqrt{7})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Find the x-intercepts: Set \(y = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} (x + 3)^2 + (0 - 2)^2 = 16 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} (x + 3)^2 + 4 = 16 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} (x + 3)^2 = 12 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} x = -3 \pm 2\sqrt{3} \end{equation*}
    x-intercepts: \((-3 + 2\sqrt{3}, 0)\) and \((-3 - 2\sqrt{3}, 0)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Circles

    Circles Video
    🔗
    A circle is the set of all points that are the same distance from a fixed point called the center.
    🔗
    Let the point \((h, k)\) be the center, some value \(r\) for the radius, and begin with the distance formula.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} r = \sqrt{(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} r^2 = (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 \end{equation*}
    This is the standard form of an equation of a circle with center \((h, k)\) and radius \(r > 0\text{.}\)
    🔗

    🔗
    • Up: \((-3, 6)\)
      🔗
    • Down: \((-3, -2)\)
      🔗
    • Left: \((-7, 2)\)
      🔗

    Subsection General Form of a Circle

    The general form of an equation of a circle is:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} x^2 + y^2 + Ax + By + C = 0 \end{equation*}

    Example 85.

    Example. Write the equation \((x + 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 16\) in general form.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Expand the squares.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} (x^2 + 6x + 9) + (y^2 - 4y + 4) = 16 \end{equation*}
    Step 2: Combine like terms and set equal to 0.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} x^2 + y^2 + 6x - 4y + 13 = 16 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} x^2 + y^2 + 6x - 4y - 3 = 0 \end{equation*}
    Therefore, the general form is \(x^2 + y^2 + 6x - 4y - 3 = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Completing the Square for Circles

    To write the equation of a circle from general form into standard form, we must complete the square twice.
    🔗

    Example 86.

    Example 1. Given \(x^2 + y^2 + 12x - 14y + 84 = 0\text{,}\) write the equation in standard form and identify the center and radius.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Group x and y terms.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} (x^2 + 12x) + (y^2 - 14y) = -84 \end{equation*}
    Step 2: Complete the square for x.
    🔗
    Half of \(12\) is \(6\text{,}\) square it: \(36\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 3: Complete the square for y.
    🔗
    Half of \(-14\) is \(-7\text{,}\) square it: \(49\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 4: Add to both sides.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} (x^2 + 12x + 36) + (y^2 - 14y + 49) = -84 + 36 + 49 \end{equation*}
    Step 5: Factor.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} (x + 6)^2 + (y - 7)^2 = 1 \end{equation*}
    Therefore, the center is \((-6, 7)\) and the radius is \(r = 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 87.

    Example 2. Complete the square to write \(4x^2 - 12x + 4y^2 + 8y = 51\) in standard form.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: Factor out the coefficient of \(x^2\) and \(y^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 4(x^2 - 3x) + 4(y^2 + 2y) = 51 \end{equation*}
    Step 2: Complete the square for x.
    🔗
    Half of \(-3\) is \(-\frac{3}{2}\text{,}\) square it: \(\frac{9}{4}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 3: Complete the square for y.
    🔗
    Half of \(2\) is \(1\text{,}\) square it: \(1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 4: Add to both sides (inside the parentheses).
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 4\left(x^2 - 3x + \frac{9}{4}\right) + 4(y^2 + 2y + 1) = 51 + 4\left(\frac{9}{4}\right) + 4(1) \end{equation*}
    Step 5: Simplify.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 4\left(x - \frac{3}{2}\right)^2 + 4(y + 1)^2 = 51 + 9 + 4 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 4\left(x - \frac{3}{2}\right)^2 + 4(y + 1)^2 = 64 \end{equation*}
    Step 6: Divide by 4.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \left(x - \frac{3}{2}\right)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 16 \end{equation*}
    Therefore, the center is \(\left(\frac{3}{2}, -1\right)\) and the radius is \(r = 4\text{.}\)
